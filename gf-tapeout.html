Two-Digit BCD Adding Calculator for Tiny Tapeout

By Michael Vert November 2026 Status: Completed

During my GlobalFoundries chip design shadowing, I designed and verified a gate-level two-digit decimal adding calculator targeted for fabrication through Tiny Tapeout. The chip accepts single-digit keypad inputs from 0 to 9, keeps a running decimal sum from 00 to 99, and drives two external 7-segment displays across 8 output pins.

Platform Tiny Tapeout (Wokwi gate-level schematic)
Inputs 4-bit BCD (0-9), key strobe, 100 Hz clock, active-low CLR
Outputs 8 pins total (two 4-bit BCD digits, 00-99)
Display Logic External combinational BCD-to-7-segment decoders

Architecture and Signal Flow

Outside the chip, a 10-button keypad and a clear button connect through 1 kÎ© pull resistors and inverters into an OR-gate encoder. Pressing any digit produces its 4-bit Binary Coded Decimal (BCD) value alongside a key strobe line. That strobe, the 4 BCD bits, a 100 Hz system clock, and an active-low clear signal all feed into the Tiny Tapeout input block.

  1. Key Edge Detector: A held button stays high for multiple clock cycles at 100 Hz, which would keep adding the same digit over and over. Two D flip-flops in series and an AND gate (Q1 AND ~Q2) turn each press into a single clock-cycle enable pulse.
  2. 4-Bit Ripple-Carry Adder: On each enable pulse, a 4-bit ripple-carry adder adds the incoming BCD digit to the current value stored in the ones register.
  3. BCD +6 Correction: Standard binary addition wraps at 16 instead of 10. A combinational check flags whenever the sum is 10 or higher, adds 6 to wrap the ones digit back into base 10, and sends a carry pulse to the tens stage.
  4. Ones Register (0-9): Four D flip-flops hold the ones digit. Each bit sits behind a 2-to-1 multiplexer controlled by the edge-detector pulse for hold or update selection, followed by synchronous AND-gate reset gating tied to active-low clear.
  5. Tens Counter (0-9): A synchronous mod-10 counter increments only when both a keypress pulse and a BCD overflow carry occur together. It rolls over from 9 back to 0 and uses the same 2-to-1 MUX and synchronous reset gating across four D flip-flops.

Both 4-bit registers route straight to the chip's 8 output pins. Outside the chip, two separate combinational decoders convert the tens and ones BCD values into individual segment lines for a pair of common-cathode 7-segment displays.

Debugging Notes

Once the schematic passed 180 parts and 340 wires, tracking down broken behavior visually on the canvas became slow. Most of the real debugging happened directly inside Wokwi's diagram.json netlist. Reading the raw JSON made it much easier to catch misrouted gate pins, check that every button had a 1000 ohm resistor and "bounce": "0" set during testing, and confirm that the reset and clock lines reached every flip-flop properly.
